## Positioning conclusion

EcoSentinel should be positioned as a **high-risk, evaluation-focused exploratory hypothesis**, not as a novel validated early-warning system. The plausible contribution, if ever supported by evidence, is narrow:

> A rigorously evaluated test of whether persistent multivariate relationship changes in high-frequency buoy data provide earlier cyanobacterial bloom warning than conventional univariate monitoring at matched false-alarm rates, using independently adjudicated biological events with interval-censored onset and explicit non-event exposure.

## What is not novel

- High-frequency in situ/probe-based cyanobacteria early warning/forecasting (Recknagel 2025; Fournier 2024).
- Cyanobacteria bloom forecasting/prediction model landscape (Rousso 2020).
- Generic early warning signals / critical transitions (Scheffer 2009; Dakos 2012; Carpenter 2011).
- Multivariate resilience indicators (Weinans 2021, metadata verified).
- Dynamical network biomarkers / network-change precursor concepts (Chen 2012, metadata verified).
- Matched-FAR evaluation as a standalone concept; it is a quality requirement, not novelty.

## What remains plausibly underexplored (unproven)

- Relationship-change/coupling indicators specifically evaluated for cyanobacterial bloom early warning **at matched FAR** against simple baselines.
- Independent biological event adjudication with interval-censored onset and verified non-event exposure.
- A Buffalo Pound-specific evaluation under that protocol (application novelty only).

## Required caution

- Do not claim "unexplored territory."
- Do not use unverified comparator leakage concerns as proof of EcoSentinel novelty.
- Do not treat natural-lake observational evidence as equivalent to experimentally manipulated evidence.
