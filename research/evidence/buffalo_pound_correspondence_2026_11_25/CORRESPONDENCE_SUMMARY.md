## 2. What Prof. Leavitt reported

### A. Saskatchewan Water Security Agency

- Over a decade of data.
- Full water chemistry at one or more sites (more in some years).
- Chlorophyll estimates (total biomass) and **some cell counts**.
- Biweekly at best, with some higher-resolution and some monthly/quarterly intervals.
- Heather Sauer is the contact.

**Status:** Most promising additional microscopy lead. Taxonomy level, biovolume/biomass, sample depth, methods, time stamps and access terms are unconfirmed.

### B. University of Regina

- Biweekly summer data (May–August) from the mid-1990s to present.
- Water chemistry and YSI/physchem profiles at one site (western basin).
- **No cell counts.**
- Chlorophyll and HPLC-analysed pigments distinguishing broad phytoplankton functional groups (diatoms, cryptophytes, cyanobacteria, N2-fixers, etc.).
- Zooplankton, sedimentary records, and remotely sensed chlorophyll.

**Status:** Useful contextual/functional-group data, but not a substitute for microscopy-derived cyanobacteria biovolume/biomass. Data dictionary and access terms unconfirmed.

### C. City of Regina Water Treatment Plant

- Data extend back to the 1970s.
- Basic water chemistry at weekly or better resolution.
- Some approximations of phytoplankton composition; lower taxonomic resolution.
- Chlorophyll values uncertain.
- Helen Baulch knows the plant collaboration best.

**Status:** Possible contextual source; unlikely to supply the independent microscopy endpoint without confirmation.

### D. University of Saskatchewan / Helen Baulch

- 15–20 years of work on the lake.
- Focus on the Water Treatment Plant dataset and high-resolution in situ sensors.
- Summer sonde deployments for about 10 years.
- Estimates of some nutrients, chlorophyll, phycobilin pigments, and standard YSI variables.
- Active research area; contact Helen directly.

**Status:** Sensor-rich. Whether paired microscopy/cell counts exist is unconfirmed. This is the key question for P3/P4.
