## 2. Email body

Dear Professor Carey,

I am writing from the EcoSentinel research project, an independent secondary-school study of whether surface chlorophyll and phycocyanin fluorescence can track independent microscopy-derived cyanobacteria abundance in lakes. We are preparing a narrow, descriptive measurement audit and would like an independent scientific opinion before any analysis is authorised. We are not asking you to endorse a result or to approve the project; we are asking whether the design is scientifically meaningful and where its limitations are fatal rather than merely disclosable.

The proposed study uses two existing, publicly documented lake datasets as separate case studies:

- **Lake Mendota, Wisconsin:** NTL-LTER daily sensor data and microscopy-derived cyanobacteria biovolume; Turner chlorophyll/phycocyanin (2006–2019) and a separate YSI era; sensors at 0.5 m, biology generally a 0–8 m composite.
- **Buffalo Pound Lake, Saskatchewan:** a 2014–2021 high-frequency buoy dataset (YSI 6025/6131 at 0.82 m) and a 2018–2019 microscopy dataset (0.8 m); two seasons only.

The proposed estimands are same-calendar-date rank associations (Spearman’s rho) between sensor fluorescence and cyanobacteria biovolume/biomass. The study is explicitly **not** a validation, calibration, agreement, prediction, bloom-detection or early-warning study. The original early-warning ambition is a conditional Stage 2 objective, not the current analysis.

The current protocol is **PROPOSED — NOT APPROVED**; all 15 project decisions are outstanding; analysis is **NOT AUTHORISED**. We are seeking scientific review, not approval.

I would be grateful for your written opinion on the following, with reasons:

1. Is the narrowed measurement question scientifically meaningful and clearly bounded, or is it merely a comparison of two imperfect measurement systems?
2. Are the biological endpoints appropriate — total cyanobacteria biovolume for Mendota and supplied cyanobacteria biomass for Buffalo Pound — given the taxonomic, preservation and detection-limit limitations?
3. Is the Mendota depth mismatch (0.5 m sensor vs 0–8 m composite biology) acceptable as a bounded limitation for a restricted case study, or does it make P1/P2 uninterpretable?
4. Does the Buffalo Pound evidence — two seasons, unresolved duplicate/`REP` semantics, derived biomass, B7 fouling intervals and possible sensor non-detection of the autumn *Planktothrix* bloom — justify retaining P3/P4 as exploratory estimates, or should they be dropped?
5. Are the proposed QC treatments defensible: exclude `C`/`E`, treat `G` and B7 as cautions rather than automatic invalidity, treat negative and >100 RFU as unresolved, and avoid a fixed coverage threshold?
6. Is the four-estimand structure unnecessarily broad? Would a Mendota-only or reduced design be more coherent?
7. Are the claims appropriately limited to rank association and non-equivalence, and are the mandatory limitations sufficient?
8. Is Stage 1 viable as a modest exploratory audit, or should it be paused pending custodian evidence?

The review would involve reading the proposed protocol, dataset descriptions, QC evidence and claims matrix. We estimate 1–2 hours. There is no fee. We will not ask you to analyse data or to take on any project role. A short written response with reasons and any conditions would be sufficient.

Before accepting, please disclose any role in the Buffalo Pound or NTL-LTER datasets, the Painter/Baulch research, or the EcoSentinel project, and any current collaboration or funding relationship that could affect independence. A disclosed conflict does not automatically disqualify you; it allows us to judge the appropriate scope.

The review materials are listed below. If you are willing, I can send the package and a short review form. If you are not available, a brief decline is perfectly fine and will not be taken as an adverse judgement.

Thank you for considering this request.

Kind regards,
