## 2. Cayelan C. Carey

**Identity and role.** Professor, Department of Biological Sciences, Virginia Tech; Co-Director, Center for Ecosystem Forecasting. Official profile: `https://biol.vt.edu/faculty/carey.html`. ORCID: `0000-0001-8835-4476`. Email verified on the official profile: `cayelan@vt.edu`.

**Relevant published work.**

- Carey et al. (2012), “Eco-physiological adaptations that favour freshwater cyanobacteria in a changing climate”, *Water Research*, `10.1016/j.watres.2011.12.016`.
- Carey et al. (2019), “A General Lake Model (GLM 3.0) for linking with high-frequency sensor data from the Global Lake Ecological Observatory Network (GLEON)”, *Geoscientific Model Development*, `10.5194/gmd-12-473-2019`.
- Carey et al. (2019), “Advancing the research agenda for improving understanding of cyanobacteria in a future of global change”, *Harmful Algae*, `10.1016/j.hal.2019.04.004`.

**Direct relevance.** Professor Carey works at the intersection of lake sensors, high-frequency data, cyanobacteria and ecological forecasting. She can assess whether a same-date rank association between surface fluorescence and microscopy-derived cyanobacteria abundance is a meaningful measurement question; whether the Mendota depth mismatch is a bounded limitation or a fatal flaw; whether the four-estimand structure is unnecessarily broad; and whether the claims matrix is sufficiently conservative. Her GLEON experience is directly relevant to the practical limitations of buoy data (coverage, fouling, calibration, sensor drift).

**Specific questions she could assess.** Scientific meaningfulness of the narrowed question; endpoint validity; sensor interpretation; depth mismatch; detection-conditioned population; four-estimand breadth; QC rules; Stage 1 proceed/reduce/pause.

**Independence and conflicts.** No direct role in the Buffalo Pound datasets or the NTL-LTER Mendota dataset was identified. She has broad collaborations through GLEON and may know the dataset authors; this is a disclosable familiarity, not a direct conflict. She should confirm that she has no current grant, supervisory or authorship relationship that would compromise independence.

**Contact status.** Verified. Suitable for the final invitation.

## 4. Petra M. Visser

**Identity and role.** Associate Professor of Algal Ecophysiology, Institute for Biodiversity and Ecosystem Dynamics, University of Amsterdam. Official profile: `https://www.uva.nl/en/profile/v/i/p.m.visser/p.m.visser.html`. ORCID: `0000-0003-3294-1908`. Email verified: `P.M.Visser@uva.nl`.

**Relevant published work.**

- Huisman et al. (2018), “Cyanobacterial blooms”, *Nature Reviews Microbiology*, `10.1038/s41579-018-0040-1` (co-author).
- Visser et al. (2007), “Summer heatwaves promote blooms of harmful cyanobacteria”, *Global Change Biology*, `10.1111/j.1365-2486.2007.01510.x`.
- Visser et al. (2015), “Artificial mixing to control cyanobacterial blooms: a review”, *Aquatic Ecology*, `10.1007/s10452-015-9537-0`.

**Direct relevance.** Dr Visser’s work on cyanobacteria physiology, buoyancy and vertical distribution is directly relevant to the central measurement problem: surface fluorescence measures a small volume at one depth, while cyanobacteria can regulate buoyancy and aggregate at the surface or at depth. She can assess whether P2/P4 (phycocyanin) are biologically interpretable and whether the depth mismatch at Mendota is acceptable.

**Specific questions she could assess.** Endpoint validity; whether surface fluorescence represents cyanobacteria abundance; morphology and vertical-distribution effects; depth mismatch; whether the four estimands should be reduced.

**Independence and conflicts.** No direct role in the two datasets identified; disclosure required.

**Contact status.** Verified. Strong alternate.
