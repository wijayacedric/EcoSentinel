# Missed Issues and Adversarial Findings

1. **Taxon absence versus zero:** The protocol requires a cyanobacteria row. It does not establish whether no such row on a sampled date means zero detected, not reported, taxonomic omission or missing biology. This may remove the most important low/absence dates and changes the estimand.
2. **Same date is not synchronous:** A date-only sample and a full-day mean may represent different water parcels and conditions. The wording acknowledges this but later “association” language could still be over-read as sensor validation.
3. **“Full-day mean” is not equivalent across lakes:** Mendota provider summaries lack within-day counts; Buffalo Pound means are computed from raw 10-minute records and then filtered at arbitrary completeness. They represent different exposures.
4. **Duplicate rows define the endpoint:** Mean-before-sum is not a harmless cleaning choice. It can reduce abundance if rows are separate counts and can be correct only if they are replicates. The alternatives are distinct ecological quantities.
5. **Selection by observability:** B7, other flags, missingness, coverage and time-of-year deployment can select nonrandom dates. Including/excluding sensitivities diagnose robustness but do not remove selection bias.
6. **Taxonomic and measurement changes:** Mendota has documented taxonomy-era changes and Turner/YSI transition. A pooled multi-year rho may blend method shifts with lake ecology. Season-year stratification does not identify a sensor effect.
7. **Pigment channel redundancy:** P1/P2 share biology and dates; P3/P4 likewise. Chlorophyll and PC are correlated optical proxies, not independent evidence. If all four receive equal headline space, the work may appear to multiply results without multiplying information.
8. **Biomass terminology:** The article supports a microscopy-derived wet-weight estimate, not direct biomass. The exact 1 g cm⁻³ factor is inferential from arithmetic consistency and common conversion, not explicitly stated in accessed article methods. State that precise limitation.
9. **Prior-art overlap:** Painter et al. already compares microscopic cyanobacteria biomass with lab chlorophyll in precisely Buffalo Pound 2018–19. The new question’s distinction (YSI in-situ fluorescence, date aggregation, all-cyano endpoint) should be explicit.
10. **Uncertainty appearance:** A 2,000-replicate percentile display can look like a confidence interval despite a “descriptive” label; it is not needed for an exploratory summary and should be dropped unless justified.
11. **No common biological scale:** Mendota biovolume and Buffalo Pound wet-weight biomass are different derivations/units. Case studies can sit under one question but there is no numerical cross-lake comparison.
12. **No research mechanism:** The proposal predicts association but does not specify expected direction by sensor/endpoint or explain conditions producing discordance. A descriptive question may not need a formal hypothesis, but the student should state what knowledge each result pattern would provide.

No alternative data search is recommended. These are resolvable by source clarification, scope, and cautious claims.
