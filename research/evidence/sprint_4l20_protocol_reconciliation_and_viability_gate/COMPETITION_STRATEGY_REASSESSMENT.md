## 3. Originality

The prior-art reassessment found **low originality**:

- Cotterill et al. (2019) directly compared a Cyclops-7 PC sensor with cyanobacteria biovolume across five lakes and a river; relationships were weak (<R²=0.4) and variable.
- The 2018 Slovenian study combined CHL and PC sensors for phytoplankton quantification and differentiation.
- The 2017 Slovenian study compared PC fluorescence with extracted PC, cell counts and biovolume.
- Loisa et al. (2015), Brient et al. (2008), Kong et al. (2013) and Hodges et al. (2017) cover PC-probe evaluation and limitations.
- Painter et al. (2022) shows sensor chlorophyll missing an autumn *Planktothrix* bloom.
- Carpenter et al. (2020, 2022), Beal et al. (2021, 2022) and O’Brien et al. (2023) cover Mendota high-frequency phycocyanin, forecasting and EWS.

The Mendota P2/P1 audit is a local long-term case study, not a new method or sensor validation. Its contribution is a transparent negative/limited result.

**Originality verdict:** **low.** It is not sufficient for an award-leading original-research entry.

## 6. Presentation potential

### Strengths

- A clear visual story: “What the sensor sees vs what the microscope sees.”
- A depth schematic: 0.5 m point vs 0–8 m composite.
- A season/year scatterplot (if authorised) showing whether the rank association is stable.
- A “what we can and cannot conclude” panel.
- A reproducibility/integrity panel.
- A negative-result narrative: “the sensor is useful, but not a substitute for microscopy; here is where it fails.”

### Risks

- The story can become a data-quality audit rather than a scientific question.
- The audience may not understand RFU, biovolume, Spearman rho or detection-conditioned populations without careful visuals.
- A null/weak result can look like “nothing happened” unless the question and implication are clear.
- The depth mismatch may dominate the judge’s questions.
- The project cannot claim early warning, so it may look disconnected from the original EcoSentinel ambition.

**Presentation verdict:** **moderate.** A strong poster is possible if it is built around one question and one visual, not the governance trail.

## 9. What would raise the ceiling

Without changing the governance status, the following would improve the project:

1. A **sharp visual question**: “Can a surface sensor substitute for whole-column microscopy?”
2. A **pre-specified negative/limited-result framing**, with a clear implication for monitoring.
3. A **season/year stability display**, not just one pooled rho.
4. A **direct comparison with Cotterill et al. (2019)**: does Mendota reproduce the weak/variable relationship, or differ?
5. A **plain student contribution statement**.
6. A **simple, well-designed poster** that explains RFU, biovolume and depth in one minute.
7. A **written licence/competition review** so the data display is compliant.

What would **not** raise the ceiling:

- adding Buffalo Pound back without custodian evidence;
- adding more estimands;
- renaming the project as AI/ML;
- claiming early warning from a same-date association;
- hiding a weak result;
- overloading the poster with governance detail.

---

## 10. Recommendation

**Proceed with the Mendota-only study only if the project owner accepts a modest, non-award-leading outcome.**

If the non-negotiable goal is an award-leading original-research project, **do not proceed with this design**. The evidence supports **pause** or a future qualified Stage 2 dataset, not a last-minute attempt to make a low-originality audit look novel.
