---

## 1. Final recommendation

**PATH 3 — Mendota-only exploratory research.**

EcoSentinel Stage 1 should become a single-lake, single-instrument-era, two-pigment descriptive measurement audit:

> **Among sampled dates in Lake Mendota during the Turner fluorometer era (2006–2019), what is the direction and magnitude of the rank association between daily mean surface phycocyanin fluorescence (0.5 m) and microscopy-derived cyanobacteria biovolume (0–8 m composite), and how does chlorophyll fluorescence compare descriptively?**

- **P2 (phycocyanin)** is the primary estimand.
- **P1 (chlorophyll)** is a secondary descriptive comparator, not a co-primary or independent confirmation.
- **Turner era only.** No YSI-era integration.
- **Mendota only.** Buffalo Pound is removed from Stage 1.
- **Descriptive only.** Spearman’s rho, n, influence diagnostics and pre-specified sensitivities. No validation, calibration, agreement, prediction, bloom detection or early warning.

This is scientifically defensible as a modest exploratory measurement audit. It is **not** sufficiently original for an award-leading competition entry. If an award-leading result is the non-negotiable goal, the correct decision is **PATH 4 — pause**, not a larger protocol.

## 5. Why this is superior to the alternatives

### Versus the current four-estimand two-lake design

- Removes the unresolvable Buffalo Pound dependency.
- Removes two incompatible endpoints (biovolume vs derived biomass).
- Removes two different licences and QC systems.
- Removes the false four-estimand replication.
- Makes the question answerable with one lake, one era, one endpoint and one licence.

### Versus Buffalo Pound as a conditional appendix

- An appendix still requires protocol space, reviewer time, QC rules and licence analysis.
- The expected scientific gain is a depth-aligned contrast that cannot be interpreted because the lakes differ in many dimensions.
- The custodian has not answered; the project should not keep a primary dependency on an unresponsive external route.

### Versus Mendota + Buffalo Pound “descriptive context”

- It would still invite two-lake comparisons and presentation ambiguity.
- The competition narrative would be harder, not stronger.
- The project has limited time; complexity is a liability.

### Versus pause now

- Mendota-only is scientifically defensible and feasible.
- The data are local, documented and CC BY 4.0.
- A negative/limited result is a legitimate contribution.
- Pause is appropriate only if the project owner requires an award-leading project or reviewers reject the depth-mismatch framing.

### Versus a new dataset hunt

- No evidence that a better dataset is accessible in time.
- Stage 2/Müggelsee remains unqualified and access-restricted.
- A new hunt would repeat the programme’s central failure: acquiring data without a qualified question.

## 6. Scientific verdict

**Defensible, but modest.**

- The question is coherent as a **surface-proxy vs integrated-biomass** audit.
- The microscopy endpoint is measurement-independent of the sensor, but not perfect.
- The depth mismatch is fundamental and cannot be corrected with the available data.
- The biology is biweekly, date-only, taxonomically non-stationary and detection-conditioned.
- The Turner record provides enough upper-bound dates for a descriptive study, but actual n is unknown.
- A pooled rho may be driven by seasonal co-trending; seasonal/influence checks are essential.
- The study cannot identify why an association is present or absent.
- It cannot support validation, calibration, prediction, bloom detection or early warning.

**Verdict:** scientifically defensible as a limited exploratory measurement audit; not a validation study and not an ecological process study.

---

## 7. Originality verdict

**Low.**

Direct prior art includes:

- Cotterill et al. (2019): Cyclops-7 phycocyanin vs cyanobacteria biovolume across lakes; weak and variable relationships.
- Loisa et al. (2015): PC fluorescence vs microscopy biomass; positive correlation with site-specific calibration.
- Brient et al. (2008): phycocyanin probe evaluation.
- Kong et al. (2013): online PC probe monitoring.
- Hodges et al. (2017): manufacturer, temperature and morphology affect PC fluorescence.
- Levi et al. (2025): sensor validation; composition, fouling, quenching and calibration effects.
- Painter et al. (2022): Buffalo Pound autumn *Planktothrix* bloom not detected by sensor chlorophyll.
- Carpenter et al. (2020, 2022), Beal et al. (2021, 2022), O’Brien et al. (2023): Mendota high-frequency phycocyanin, forecasting and EWS prior work.

The proposed Mendota P2/P1 audit is a **local case study**, not a new method or a new sensor validation. Its contribution is a transparent, pre-specified negative/limited result. That is a legitimate but low-originality contribution.

## 9. Strongest fatal or critical concern

**The Mendota depth mismatch is the critical scientific concern.** A 0.5 m surface sensor is compared with a 0–8 m integrated biovolume. The data contain no depth-resolved biology to quantify the mismatch. The study can describe a surface-proxy vs whole-column association, but it cannot validate the sensor, estimate surface biomass, or explain whether a weak association is due to depth, taxonomy, quenching, season or sampling.

This is **fatal** to validation/EWS claims; it is **remediable only by reframing** as a surface-vs-composite measurement audit, with reviewer acceptance.

The second critical concern is **low originality**. The design is a local case study of a well-studied relationship. It cannot be made award-leading by better writing or more sensitivities.

## 12. Stage 2 implications

- The original early-warning ambition is **not** addressed by the Mendota-only Stage 1 audit.
- Mendota is **not** a Stage 2 dataset for daily EWS: biology is biweekly, 0–8 m composite, date-only, and lacks event labels.
- Buffalo Pound is **not** a Stage 2 dataset: two seasons, unresolved provenance.
- Müggelsee remains the strongest public candidate: long-term weekly microscopy (FRED 867), weekly probe (FRED 1) and high-frequency sensors (FRED 1145), but access is all rights reserved, the sensor station/depth differ from the biological stations, and no public event definition or calibration/QC record exists.
- Stage 2 remains **conditional and unqualified**. No dataset is qualified.
