## 4. Balanced evidence synthesis

The 4L.12 review leaned toward “weak/variable.” The full evidence is **mixed**:

### 4.1 Positive/strong findings

- **Rozina et al. 2018:** high correlation of CHL and PC fluorescence with biovolume in two small water bodies over two years, despite seasonal succession.
- **Thomson-Laing et al. 2020:** strong overall relationships (r² ≥ 0.78) in environmental samples; lake-specific 0.06–0.90.
- **Rogora et al. 2023:** total biovolume R² = 0.72 for Cyclops7 vs 0–20 m microscopy.
- **Hartmann et al. 2019:** good total chlorophyll–total biovolume agreement, with group-specific bias.
- **Ma et al. 2023:** 2-h moving-average PC vs total cyanobacterial biovolume R = 0.73 (p = 0.098) at a high-risk plant.
- **Haggard et al. 2023:** raw fluorescence thresholds associated with microcystin risk at a recreational lake.

### 4.2 Negative/weak findings

- **Bowling et al. 2016:** PC good in 2/3 ponds; poor with picoplankton; PC vs cell counts poor; chlorophyll poor for cyanobacteria.
- **Hodges et al. 2018:** field PC–biovolume R² = 0.22–0.29 for two sensors; non-significant for a third; morphology/temperature effects.
- **Cotterill et al. 2019:** weak/variable relationships (<R² 0.4); species-specific detection.
- **USGS SIR 2024-5010:** PC vs cyanobacterial biovolume not significant; chlorophyll vs total biovolume weaker; temperature/turbidity more informative.
- **Kovalenko et al. 2026:** cyanobacteria/green/crypto weak; diatoms strongest; seasonal/deep-chlorophyll differences.
- **Ma et al. 2023:** no meaningful correlation at low-risk plants; negative readings below detection.
- **Painter et al. 2022:** autumn *Planktothrix* >60% biomass missed by sensor/lab chlorophyll-a.

### 4.3 Inconclusive/conditional findings

- **Levi et al. 2025:** calibration, fouling, composition, nighttime, and high-frequency events affect performance; no universal conversion.
- **Bertone et al. 2018/2019:** calibration/turbidity/species-composition challenges; species-specific accuracy.
- **Rousso et al. 2021:** quenching up to 79% chl, 59% PC; depends on light/temperature.
- **USGS SIR 2022-5103:** PhytoFind overestimated chlorophyll; group % correlation variable.
- **Carpenter et al. 2020/2022:** Mendota HF PC has stochastic states, variable lags/extremes, sensor regime changes.

### 4.4 Correct synthesis

> Fluorescence–abundance relationships are **mixed and context-dependent**: sometimes strong, often weak, and sensitive to taxon/morphology, depth/sample integration, season, temperature/light, instrument/calibration, fouling, and temporal aggregation.

The protocol must not claim “uniformly weak” or “validated.” It must report what the two case studies find and state the conditions.

## 5. What the literature establishes

1. Total chlorophyll often tracks total phytoplankton biovolume reasonably well; group-specific relationships are weaker.
2. Phycocyanin–cyanobacteria relationships are mixed; sometimes strong, often weak.
3. Taxonomy/morphology matters (colonial *Microcystis*, picocyanobacteria, *Planktothrix*).
4. Depth/sample integration matters.
5. Season and deep chlorophyll layers matter.
6. Diel quenching matters.
7. Instrument/calibration/fouling matter.
8. Temporal aggregation matters.
9. Negative/contradictory results are common, but positive results exist.

## 6. What the literature does not establish

- A universal RFU-to-biovolume conversion.
- A sensor accurate across taxa, lakes, seasons, depths.
- That a surface sensor represents whole-column biomass.
- That a rank correlation validates a sensor.
- That the proposed two-case study is novel.

## 7. Originality assessment

**LOW–MODERATE.**

The study:

- asks a well-studied measurement question;
- adds two specific lakes and a depth-mismatch contrast;
- uses standard descriptive statistics;
- does not add a new sensor, calibration, method, or generalisable dataset;
- can produce a useful negative/limited local measurement audit.

It is sufficient for a rigorous educational/competition measurement study if a negative/limited result is acceptable. It is not sufficient for a genuinely novel early-warning claim. If novelty is required, **PAUSE**.
