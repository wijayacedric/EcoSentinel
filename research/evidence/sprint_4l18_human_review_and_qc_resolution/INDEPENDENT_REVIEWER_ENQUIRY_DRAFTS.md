## Draft 1 — Scientific reviewer

**Subject:** Request for independent scientific review — EcoSentinel freshwater measurement study

Dear [Reviewer name],

I am a secondary-school student in Ireland developing an independent freshwater ecology research project, EcoSentinel. I am seeking an independent scientific review of the study design and its limits, not an endorsement of a result.

The project compares surface chlorophyll and phycocyanin fluorescence with independent microscopy-derived cyanobacteria abundance in Lake Mendota and Buffalo Pound Lake. The current question is deliberately narrow: a descriptive, same-calendar-date rank-association study, analysed as two separate lake-specific case studies. It is not an early-warning validation, sensor calibration, or predictive study.

The data have substantial limitations: date-only microscopy, a 0.5 m sensor versus a 0–8 m composite at Mendota, a Turner/YSI discontinuity, two Buffalo Pound seasons, unresolved duplicate-row and biomass provenance issues, and incomplete QC documentation. The protocol is **PROPOSED — NOT APPROVED**, and no analysis is authorised.

I would be grateful for your written opinion on:

- whether the narrowed question is scientifically meaningful and clearly bounded;
- whether the four estimands should remain exploratory or be reduced;
- whether the detection-conditioned population is acceptable;
- whether the depth mismatch is handled honestly;
- whether the proposed QC exclusions are conservative and defensible;
- whether Stage 1 is viable as a modest exploratory audit or should be paused.

I am not asking you to approve the protocol, endorse the hypothesis, or take on the project. A short written opinion with reasons and any conditions would be sufficient. I can provide the protocol, addendum, decision matrix and evidence package.

If you have a conflict of interest — for example, involvement in the datasets or the Painter/Baulch research — please let me know.

Kind regards,

Cedric Wijaya

## Draft 2 — Statistical reviewer

**Subject:** Request for independent statistical review — EcoSentinel date-level rank study

Dear [Reviewer name],

I am a secondary-school student in Ireland developing an independent freshwater ecology research project, EcoSentinel. I am seeking an independent statistical review of a descriptive date-level study, not an endorsement of a result.

The project proposes Spearman’s rho between surface chlorophyll/phycocyanin fluorescence and independent microscopy-derived cyanobacteria abundance in Lake Mendota and Buffalo Pound Lake. The design is two separate case studies, with no pooling, no p-values, no bootstrap confidence interval, and no prediction. Mendota has 14-day date-only biology and a 0.5 m versus 0–8 m depth mismatch; Buffalo Pound has two seasons and variable daily sensor coverage. The protocol is **PROPOSED — NOT APPROVED**, and no analysis is authorised.

I would be grateful for your written opinion on:

- whether Spearman’s rho is defensible for this descriptive question;
- whether the proposed descriptive sensitivity ranges are honestly labelled;
- whether leave-one-year-out / leave-one-date-out are adequate influence diagnostics;
- what overall minimum-estimability rule should be set before a count-only audit;
- whether the detection-conditioned population and QC exclusions are likely to bias rho;
- whether the four estimates and multiple sensitivities create hidden multiplicity or selection problems;
- whether Stage 1 is statistically viable as a descriptive audit or should be paused.

I am not asking you to approve D-15, endorse a result, or validate the protocol as a whole. A short written opinion with reasons and conditions would be sufficient. I can provide the protocol, uncertainty plan, decision matrix and evidence package.

If you have a conflict of interest — for example, involvement in the datasets or the NTL-LTER/Painter/Baulch programmes — please let me know.

Kind regards,

Cedric Wijaya  
EcoSentinel — independent student research project
