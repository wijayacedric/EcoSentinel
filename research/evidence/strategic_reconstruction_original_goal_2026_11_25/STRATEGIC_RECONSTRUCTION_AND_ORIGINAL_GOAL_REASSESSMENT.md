## A. Executive verdict

### Primary recommendation: **C — develop a sequential research programme**

**Stage 1 (now):** Complete the narrowed measurement/comparability study already specified in Sprint 4L.14, after the independent-review addendum and the required human approvals. This is the only scientifically defensible project that can be completed with verified data in hand.

**Stage 2 (conditional):** Return to the original early-warning question only if a dataset is acquired that meets the minimum evidence standard in Section F — at least five years, at least ten independently defined bloom events, time-stamped independent microscopy at roughly weekly or better resolution, co-located high-frequency multivariate sensors, documented non-bloom periods, and sensor calibration metadata.

### The central question answered

**We are missing the data, not the information.**

The project now has enough information to know precisely why the original question cannot be tested with the current datasets. More metadata, more literature, or more correspondence about Buffalo Pound and Mendota will not convert those datasets into an early-warning validation. The binding constraint is the structure of the available data: two seasons at Buffalo Pound, fortnightly date-only microscopy at Mendota, mismatched depths, no independently adjudicated bloom/non-bloom labels, and no non-bloom exposure denominator.

That is different from saying the original question is scientifically impossible. It is not. Long-term programmes with paired high-frequency sensors and independent microscopy exist — Müggelsee is the clearest candidate identified — but none is currently verified, accessible, or analysed in this project. The original ambition is therefore **delayed and conditional, not dead**.

### Key findings

1. **There is no Sprint 4L.15 package in the repository.** The latest completed work is the Sprint 4L.14 independent review (2026-11-25) and the Buffalo Pound correspondence package (2026-11-25). The pivot recommendation described in the brief is not traceable to any repository file, evidence register, search log, or decision record. It cannot be treated as authoritative project state.
2. **The original goal is not testable with current data.** Buffalo Pound has 42 microscopy dates across two seasons and no verified non-bloom baseline. Mendota has 402 dates over 26 years but a 14-day median cadence, date-only samples, a 0.5 m sensor versus 0–8 m composite mismatch, and a Turner/YSI discontinuity.
3. **The narrowing decision was correct for the data in hand, but the external search was incomplete.** The project systematically investigated Buffalo Pound, Mendota, Greifensee and Western Lake Erie, but the repository contains no evidence that several long-term paired lake programmes were assessed. That is a missed opportunity, not a reason to reverse the narrowing retroactively.
4. **A pivot to a student-led field study would not solve the original problem.** It would test sensor–community–biovolume relationships over one season, not early warning. It is scientifically coherent but incremental, resource-intensive, and — if SciFest 2027 is in spring 2027 — chronologically incapable of producing results in time.
5. **The strongest achievable 2027 project is a rigorous two-lake measurement audit with an explicit data-sufficiency conclusion.** It will not be a top-tier original discovery, but it can be defensible, reproducible, and honest. Its ceiling is higher if the student owns the question and the negative/limited result is presented as a real scientific finding rather than an apology.

### What this report does not do

It does not approve the protocol, change Gate 2, resolve any decision, authorise D-15, acquire data, contact researchers, or begin analysis. It recommends; it does not govern.

## C. Original research objective reconstruction

### C.1 The original question

> **Can persistent changes in relationships between freshwater environmental variables provide earlier warning of cyanobacterial blooms than conventional monitoring, at comparable false-alarm rates?**

Source: `README.md`; `documentation/SCIENTIFIC_PROTOCOL_DRAFT.md`.

### C.2 Original scientific hypothesis

The project's original hypothesis can be reconstructed as follows:

- Cyanobacterial blooms are preceded by a change in the **coupling** among environmental variables — for example, dissolved oxygen, pH, temperature, turbidity, chlorophyll fluorescence, phycocyanin fluorescence, PAR, and conductivity.
- A bloom is not merely a rise in one pigment signal; it is a **regime shift** in the lake's coupled physics–chemistry–biology.
- Persistent changes in the relationships among those variables (rolling correlations, covariance structure, multivariate distance, variance) may therefore appear **before** the bloom is visible in a conventional single-variable threshold.
- A relational indicator could provide **additional lead time** at a **comparable false-alarm rate** to conventional chlorophyll/phycocyanin monitoring.

The ecological mechanism is plausible but not automatic. Bloom onset can be driven by nutrient pulses, stratification, wind mixing, thermal fronts, grazing, and vertical migration. A relational change may be a cause, a consequence, or an unrelated seasonal transition. That is why independent biological ground truth and a non-event denominator are essential.

### C.3 Intended prediction and evaluation

The intended evaluation was not "does a sensor correlate with biomass?" It was an **event-level early-warning comparison**:

| Element | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Bloom event | Independently defined onset, ideally from microscopy/biovolume, not from the predictor signal |
| Non-event | Periods sampled with the same method where no bloom occurred |
| Warning horizon | Pre-specified lead time *H* (days/weeks) |
| Persistence rule | Pre-specified duration/number of intervals for a "persistent" relational change |
| Conventional baseline | Univariate chlorophyll/phycocyanin/turbidity/DO threshold or rule |
| Matched FAR | Both methods compared at the same false-alarm rate, or full ROC/PR operating curve |
| Event-level validation | Hold out years/events; no random row splits; no leakage |
| Uncertainty | Interval-censored onset; event-level resampling or equivalent |

### C.4 Proposed variables and ground truth

**Environmental variables in the intended design:** temperature, dissolved oxygen, pH, conductivity, turbidity, chlorophyll RFU, phycocyanin RFU, PAR, meteorology; nutrients where available.

**Biological ground truth:** independent microscopy-derived cyanobacteria biovolume or biomass, with species/genus resolution, sample date/time, depth, method, units, detection limits, and QC. The endpoint must be independent of the sensor predictor. Chlorophyll or phycocyanin fluorescence cannot serve as both predictor and ground truth.

### C.6 Why the project narrowed

Sprint 4L.11 narrowed the question because:

- Buffalo Pound had only two biological seasons, ~42 dates, one station/depth, and no verified non-bloom exposure.
- Mendota had 14-day biology, date-only samples, a depth mismatch, and a sensor-era discontinuity; it was rejected for early-warning validation in 4L.10.
- No other candidate was confirmatory-ready.
- Prior art already contained high-frequency HAB early warning/forecasting and EWS evaluations with true/false alarm rates.
- A descriptive measurement question could be answered with existing verified data without new access, labels, models, or contacts.

That reasoning remains valid **for the data then in hand**. Its weak point is the implicit premise that the current four candidates were the relevant universe. The repository's search record does not demonstrate that this premise was tested; the external search in Section E identifies other long-term paired programmes, though none is currently accessible.

### C.7 Fatal limitations versus missing information

This distinction is central to the brief. The table separates problems that genuinely block the original question from problems that merely block the current protocol.

| Type | Item | Does it block the original EWS question? | Can it be fixed by more information/metadata? |

**Conclusion:** The original goal was rejected because the necessary **evidence had not been obtained**, not because the hypothesis was shown to be false or impossible. But the missing evidence is primarily **data**, not explanatory information. The project has spent substantial effort learning *why* the current data are insufficient; that is valuable, but it does not substitute for the data themselves.

One concrete example of the distinction: Sprint 4L.14 stated that the Painter et al. (2022) full text was inaccessible (`UPDATED_HUMAN_DECISION_REGISTER.md`, D-02). The independent review subsequently accessed the publisher article and verified that microscopy enumeration was converted to wet-weight biomass using cell-volume geometry. The protocol has not yet been updated to reflect this. That is a genuine information gap that can be fixed — and fixing it improves Stage 1, but it does not create the multi-year, time-stamped, non-bloom observations that the original question requires.

### E.3 Dataset-by-dataset assessment

#### Buffalo Pound Lake

**Strengths.** It is the only fully verified system with independent species-level microscopy, cell density, biomass, and a same-depth (0.8 m) high-frequency buoy. All 42 biological dates match buoy days. The data are public and integrity-registered. It is the best Stage 1 case study.

**Fatal weakness for the original question.** Two seasons, 42 dates, one station/depth, and no verified non-bloom exposure. The 2018 and 2019 seasons are both bloom seasons; the median cyanophyte share is high. There is no winter/spring baseline. A 16-day maximum gap cannot bound bloom onset below the claimed warning horizon. The 2019-07-17 anomaly remains unresolved. The 2018/2019 taxonomy and schema differ (73 vs 56 taxa; 39 shared; `Sample_ID` vs `REP`).

**Verdict.** Exploratory measurement case study; not an EWS validation dataset. WSA data could change this verdict only if it supplies multi-year, time-stamped, species-level counts with non-bloom sampling and site co-location.

#### Lake Mendota

**Strengths.** 402 dates over 26 years, species-level microscopy, direct biovolume and cell density, daily sensors from 2006, and a genuine independent biological endpoint. It is the strongest long-term biology in hand.

**Fatal weaknesses for the original question.** Median 14-day cadence, date-only samples, 0.5 m sensors versus 0–8 m tube composites, ~106 m spatial offset, station blank for 371/402 dates, and a Turner/YSI discontinuity with order-of-magnitude differences and no crosswalk. Only 64/402 dates have the joint Turner core; 2/402 have the joint YSI core. The 4L.10 independent challenge explicitly rejected Mendota for early-warning validation.

**Verdict.** Retain for limited exploratory measurement comparison; reject for original EWS validation. More Mendota metadata will not fix the 14-day biology or the depth mismatch.

#### Greifensee

**Strengths.** Five years of sub-hourly imagery, 60/60 packages, rich environmental pairing.

**Fatal weakness.** The biological endpoint is classifier-derived, not independent microscopy. There is no calibrated cell density or biovolume, no independent validation, and four schema/classifier versions. It cannot serve as ground truth for a sensor or EWS question.

**Verdict.** Exploratory context only; not a biological reference.

#### Western Lake Erie

**Strengths.** Long record, public NOAA archive, microcystin data, weekly/biweekly sampling.

**Fatal weaknesses.** No cell counts or biovolume described; pigments/toxins are proxies, not independent cell biomass. No co-located high-frequency multivariate buoy demonstrated. NCEI endpoints returned 503; no raw files were obtained.

**Verdict.** Not viable with current evidence.

#### Lake Erken

**Strengths.** Open, species-level phytoplankton biomass from 1989–2011, weekly during the ice-free season. Long-term temperature/oxygen and a high-frequency chlorophyll record from 2019.

**Critical gap.** The 1989–2011 biology does not overlap the 2019–2025 high-frequency chlorophyll record. The older high-frequency sensor variables are not verified. Without a paired multivariate sensor period, it cannot test the original relational question.

**Verdict.** Potential contextual comparison; not a current EWS candidate.

#### Lake Müggelsee

**Strengths.** The strongest external candidate identified. Phytoplankton biomass/biovolume from 1979–ongoing; high-frequency multiparameter probe at 1.5 m from 2001–ongoing, 5-min cadence, with phycocyanin explicitly listed and chlorophyll/oxygen/turbidity/conductivity/pH reported in associated literature. A 20+ year overlap of independent biology and high-frequency sensors is exactly the structure the original question needs.

**Critical gaps.** The data are **all rights reserved**, not open; a request to IGB (Sabine Hilt / Tom Shatwell) is required. The phytoplankton sampling cadence, depth, taxonomic resolution, and event/non-event coverage are not verified. The sensor QC and calibration are not verified. Access may be granted for student research, but it is not guaranteed.

**Verdict.** Highest potential for Stage 2; not currently usable. A metadata request is justified, but it should not displace Stage 1.

#### Lake Kasumigaura

**Strengths.** Monthly/biweekly phytoplankton since 1978; long-term biovolume.

**Critical gap.** Co-located high-frequency multivariate sensor data are not verified. Without them, the relational question cannot be tested.

**Verdict.** Screening only; do not invest further unless a paired sensor archive is confirmed.

### E.4 Integration and pooling

No defensible pooled dataset can be built from these sources:

- Mendota reports biovolume; Buffalo Pound reports biomass; Greifensee reports classifier labels; WLE reports pigments/toxins.
- Depths differ (0–8 m composite, 0.8 m, 1.5 m, 3 m).
- Instruments differ (Turner vs YSI; different fluorometers; different cameras).
- Cadence differs (10 min, daily, 14 days, weekly, monthly).
- Taxonomy differs (species-level but non-stationary; 2018/2019 schema break; classifier labels).

A cross-lake synthesis must be a **side-by-side descriptive contrast**, not a pooled correlation or a combined model. Adding lakes to increase *n* would create a false sample size and confound system, method, and depth.

## F. Scientific and statistical feasibility

### F.1 Minimum evidence standard for the original question

A valid early-warning evaluation requires all of the following. The table separates what current data provide from what a qualifying external dataset would need.

| ID | Requirement | Why it is necessary | Current data | Qualifying dataset |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | Independent biological bloom definition | Avoids circularity with sensor predictors | **Fail** — no approved definition | Expert-adjudicated, microscopy-based |
| R2 | Sampled non-bloom periods | Provides false-alarm denominator | **Fail** — no verified non-bloom exposure | Same method in non-bloom seasons |
| R3 | Time-stamped biology | Bounds onset and lead time | **Fail** — Mendota date-only; BP date-only | Date + time, ideally depth |
| R4 | Biological cadence ≤ claimed lead time | Avoids interval censoring larger than the effect | **Fail** — 14 d Mendota; 4–16 d BP | Weekly or better |
| R5 | Co-located sensor and biology | Avoids spatial mismatch | **Partial** — BP same depth, site proximity to verify; Mendota offset/depth mismatch | Same station/depth or profiles |
| R6 | Multi-year coverage with multiple events | Enables event-level holdout and stable FAR | **Fail** — BP 2 seasons; Mendota 26 y but 14 d | ≥5 years, ≥10 events |
| R7 | Sensor calibration / era metadata | Distinguishes artefact from ecology | **Partial/fail** — no per-record calibration; Turner/YSI break | Documented calibration and maintenance |
| R8 | Pre-specified alert rule and persistence threshold | Prevents post hoc optimisation | **Fail** — not approved | Pre-registered protocol |
| R9 | Matched-FAR comparison with conventional baseline | Tests the actual claim | **Fail** — no denominator/events | Event-level ROC/PR or matched FAR |
| R10 | Leakage, seasonality, and autocorrelation controls | Prevents inflated performance | **Not yet designed** | Year/event holdout; seasonal nulls; block resampling |

**Conclusion:** Current data fail R1–R4 and R6, and only partially satisfy R5/R7. R8–R10 are design requirements that could be specified, but without R1–R6 they have nothing valid to evaluate.

### F.2 Why more metadata will not fix the current datasets

The missing-metadata items — biomass formula, replicate semantics, QC flags, taxonomy crosswalk, station identity, anomaly explanation — are real and must be resolved for Stage 1. But even a perfect resolution would leave:

- 42 Buffalo Pound dates over two seasons;
- 402 Mendota dates at a 14-day median;
- no sampled non-bloom periods at Buffalo Pound;
- no time-of-day for Mendota;
- no depth-resolved pairing at either lake;
- no independent event adjudication.

Those are properties of the data collection design, not of the documentation. No amount of metadata can create observations that were never made.

### F.3 Statistical consequences

| Issue | Consequence for the original question |
|---|---|
| Interval-censored onset | Lead time can only be reported as an interval, often wider than the claimed warning horizon |
| Event dependence | Repeated sensor readings are not independent; event-level *n* is the number of blooms, not rows |
| Seasonal confounding | Relational changes may track spring warming or autumn mixing, not bloom onset |
| Autocorrelation | Effective sample size is much smaller than the nominal series length |
| Sensor eras | Turner/YSI differences are orders of magnitude; concatenation would create a false regime shift |
| Multiple indicators | Testing many relational metrics without pre-registration inflates false positives |
| Non-event exposure | Without sampled non-events, false-alarm rate is undefined |
| Class imbalance | Blooms are rare; accuracy is misleading; event-level sensitivity/specificity are required |
| Holdout design | Random row splits leak future information; year/event holdout is mandatory |

### F.4 What a valid study would look like

A defensible Stage 2 study would:

1. Use one lake with a documented, co-located, multi-year paired record.
2. Define bloom events from independent microscopy/biovolume using pre-registered criteria, with sensitivity to alternative thresholds.
3. Define non-event periods from the same sampling method, including winter/spring where available.
4. Bound onset with interval censoring and report lead time only where it exceeds the sampling interval.
5. Pre-specify one or a small number of relational indicators and a persistence rule.
6. Compare against conventional chlorophyll/phycocyanin thresholds at matched FAR.
7. Hold out whole years or events; use seasonal nulls and block resampling.
8. Report event-level sensitivity, false-alarm rate, lead-time distribution, and negative results.

This is a feasible study with the right data. It is not feasible with the current data.

## G. Sprint 4L.15 reassessment

### G.1 Provenance

No Sprint 4L.15 package exists in the repository. The recommendation described in the brief — pivot to a student-led field study on cyanobacterial community composition, environmental conditions, and the phycocyanin–biovolume relationship — has no:

- evidence register;
- search log;
- protocol;
- feasibility assessment;
- resource plan;
- decision record;
- governance status.

It is therefore an **external proposal**, not verified project state. It also conflicts with the 4L.11 decision, which said a pivot was not recommended absent a specific, evidence-based opportunity.

### G.2 What the proposed field study would actually test

The described question is essentially:

> How do cyanobacterial community composition and environmental conditions modify the relationship between phycocyanin fluorescence and microscopy-derived biovolume?

That is a **measurement/calibration question**, not an early-warning question. It could produce:

- a local species-specific calibration;
- evidence that PC fluorescence per biovolume varies with community composition;
- a better understanding of interference and detection limits;
- a useful negative/limited result.

It could not produce:

- bloom onset timing;
- lead time;
- false-alarm rates;
- a matched-FAR comparison;
- a test of relational early warning.

### G.3 Scientific merit

**Strengths:**

- Direct paired sampling, which the existing datasets lack.
- Student ownership of field and laboratory work.
- Potential to characterize local community effects.
- Could generate a genuinely new local dataset.

**Weaknesses:**

- The core question is already well studied: species-specific PC fluorescence, cell-size effects, pigment content, DOM interference, and sensor-specific calibration are documented (Bastien et al. 2011, DOI 10.1039/C0EM00366B; Bastien et al. 2019, PubMed 31349889; Cotterill et al. 2019, DOI 10.1080/00288330.2019.1617322; Thomson-Laing et al. 2020, PubMed 32732055).
- Painter et al. 2022 already pairs microscopy biomass and chlorophyll in Buffalo Pound and shows taxon/phase-dependent relationships.
- A one-season field study cannot establish generality or early warning.
- Without replication across lakes/seasons, it is a local calibration, not a transferable finding.

### G.4 Feasibility and timeline

| Requirement | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Field site access | Not documented |
| Boat, sonde, sampling equipment | Not documented |
| Microscopy laboratory and taxonomic expertise | Not documented |
| Permits/ethics | Not documented |
| Funding | Not documented |
| Duration | At least one ice-free season for sampling, plus lab and analysis |
| SciFest 2027 timing | If the event is spring 2027, a summer 2027 field season is too late |
| Supervision | A taxonomist/microscopist is essential; not documented |

This is not a criticism of the idea's scientific logic. It is a criticism of treating an un-resourced, un-timed proposal as a safer alternative to a study that can start now with verified data.

### G.5 Competition value

A well-executed field study could be more engaging to judges than a reanalysis of old data, because it shows original fieldwork and student ownership. But:

- it is a crowded measurement literature;
- one season gives weak inference;
- the timeline is risky;
- the result may be a local calibration curve with limited novelty.

It is not obviously stronger than a rigorous two-lake measurement audit, and it is clearly weaker for the original early-warning ambition.

### G.6 Verdict

**Do not adopt the field study as the primary pivot.** It is:

- not an early-warning study;
- not feasible for a spring 2027 competition unless the field season is already underway;
- not documented or resourced;
- not a substitute for the original question.

It could be:

- a **Stage 1b** add-on if a local site, supervisor, and lab are secured;
- a **future-year** project if the competition timeline is later;
- a **complementary calibration study** if the Stage 1 measurement audit identifies a specific local gap.

It fails the 4L.11 test for a pivot: there is no specific, evidence-based opportunity with a credible execution plan.

## H. Best achievable research direction

### H.1 The two-stage programme

**Stage 1 — Measurement reliability and data sufficiency (now).**

**Primary question:**

> In Lake Mendota (2006–2020) and Buffalo Pound Lake (2018–2019), among same-day paired observations and analysed separately by lake and sensor era, how strongly and consistently do surface chlorophyll and phycocyanin fluorescence rank-track independent microscopy-derived cyanobacteria biovolume/biomass, and how do those associations vary by season and by the depth-alignment setting?

**Contribution:** A transparent, reproducible two-lake measurement audit that:

- quantifies the direction, strength, and descriptive uncertainty of the rank association;
- documents where surface fluorescence diverges from depth-integrated microscopy;
- separates sensor eras and lake systems rather than pooling them;
- states plainly that the result is not a validation, calibration, or early-warning test;
- specifies the data conditions required for a future early-warning evaluation.

**Data:** Mendota 88.31 + 129.42; Buffalo Pound Painter 2018–2019 + FRDR buoy. No new access required.

**Scope controls:** same-day primary; ±1-day exploratory only if approved; Spearman ρ only; no Pearson/log-Pearson; no relative concordance; descriptive sensitivity ranges; no bootstrap interval for Buffalo Pound; no labels, models, thresholds, or event intervals.

**Why it is the best use of the current position:** It is the only study that can be completed with verified data, and it directly addresses the actual blocker — whether surface sensor proxies can represent independent cyanobacteria abundance. A negative or limited result is a genuine contribution if framed honestly.

**Stage 2 — Early-warning evaluation (conditional).**

**Primary question:**

> Across at least five years and at least ten independently defined bloom events, do persistent changes in the relationships among high-frequency environmental variables detect bloom onset earlier than conventional chlorophyll/phycocyanin thresholds at matched false-alarm rates?

**Data gate:** all of R1–R7 in Section F must be satisfied, plus an approved R8–R10 design.

**Candidate sources:** Müggelsee first; WSA Buffalo Pound second; any other programme that can document co-located, time-stamped, multi-year, independent microscopy and high-frequency multivariate sensors.

**Why it is conditional:** No such dataset is currently verified or accessible. Committing to Stage 2 now would repeat the original error — assuming data that do not exist.

### H.2 Why not the alternatives

| Option | Assessment | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Recover original now with additional information** | Reject for 2027 | No verified qualifying dataset; access and cadence unknown; timeline insufficient |
| **B. Retain original but narrow endpoint/period/validation** | Reject as primary | Narrowing the endpoint does not fix 14-day date-only biology, depth mismatch, or missing non-events |
| **C. Sequential programme** | **Adopt** | Preserves the original ambition while securing a defensible 2027 project now |
| **D. Replace datasets while preserving original objective** | Conditional sub-option of C | Müggelsee is the best candidate, but access is unverified; do not bet the project on it |
| **E. Abandon original and pursue field study** | Reject | Not early warning; timeline and resource risk; incremental literature |
| **F. Abandon programme** | Reject | Stage 1 is feasible and scientifically defensible; no need to abandon |

### H.3 What would justify upgrading to Stage 2

A dataset would need to demonstrate, with documentation:

1. At least five years of overlap between independent microscopy/biovolume and high-frequency sensors.
2. At least ten independently defined bloom events, plus sampled non-bloom periods.
3. Time-stamped sampling at roughly weekly or better resolution during bloom season.
4. Co-located sensor and biology at a comparable depth, or depth-resolved profiles.
5. Documented sensor calibration, maintenance, and QC.
6. Access terms that permit the intended use.
7. A feasible route to expert event adjudication and statistical review.

If Müggelsee or WSA satisfies these by the Stage 2 gate, the original question can be recovered. If not, it remains a long-term aspiration.

### H.4 What not to do

- Do not pool Mendota and Buffalo Pound to increase *n*.
- Do not use chlorophyll or phycocyanin as both predictor and bloom label.
- Do not treat classifier labels as independent biology.
- Do not call a rank association "validation" or "agreement".
- Do not run correlations before D-15.
- Do not describe the field study as an early-warning test.
- Do not treat the absence of a reply as evidence that data do not exist.
- Do not treat the presence of a dataset as evidence that it is usable.

### K.1 Recommendation

**C — sequential research programme.**

Stage 1 is the current narrowed measurement study, completed after the 4L.14 addendum and all required approvals. Stage 2 is the original early-warning question, pursued only if a qualifying dataset is acquired and verified.

### K.2 Confidence

- **High confidence** that the current datasets cannot support the original early-warning question. The evidence is consistent across 4F, 4L.9, 4L.10, 4L.11, and the 4L.14 independent review.
- **Moderate–high confidence** that Stage 1 is the best available 2027 project. It is feasible, defensible, and uses verified data.
- **Moderate confidence** that Stage 2 can eventually be recovered. Müggelsee is a credible candidate, but access and cadence are unverified.
- **Low–moderate confidence** that Stage 2 can be completed by spring 2027 even if access is granted.

### K.3 Key assumptions

1. SciFest 2027 is a spring 2027 event; if not, the field-study timeline changes.
2. No qualifying dataset arrives before January 2027.
3. Stage 1 approvals can be obtained in December 2026–January 2027.
4. The student can commit to a strict descriptive protocol.
5. The current data hashes and integrity records remain unchanged.

### K.4 Evidence that would change the decision

**Toward A/D (recover original now):**

- Müggelsee or WSA provides verified multi-year, time-stamped, species-level biovolume with co-located high-frequency multivariate sensors, sampled non-bloom periods, and at least ten events — **before** the Stage 2 gate.
- A documented sensor calibration and event-adjudication plan is in place.
- Access terms permit the intended use.

**Toward E (field study):**

- SciFest 2027 is late 2027 or 2028.
- A field site, boat, sonde, microscopy lab, taxonomist, permits, and funding are secured by January 2027.

- Stage 1 approvals are refused or indefinitely delayed.
- The student cannot commit to the descriptive scope.
- Licence/competition review prohibits the intended use.

### K.5 Final answer to the central question

**We are missing the data necessary to make the original project scientifically possible, not merely the information needed to complete it.**

The project has done the hard work of establishing *why* the current data are insufficient. That is a real achievement. But the next step is not another metadata sprint, another protocol revision, or a pivot to a field study that cannot deliver in time. The next step is to secure the best defensible 2027 project with the data in hand, while keeping a disciplined, evidence-gated path back to the original ambition.

The original goal is not lost. It is **conditional**.
