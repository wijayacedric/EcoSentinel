## 3. Viability of the original question

| Requirement | Status |
|---|---|
| Biological validity — defensible bloom events and non-bloom exposure | **Not satisfied** |
| Temporal validity — resolve onset and lead time | **Not satisfied** |
| Measurement validity — reliable, comparable, aligned sensors | **Partially satisfied** |
| Evaluation validity — matched FAR with leakage-resistant validation | **Not satisfied** |
| Statistical feasibility — enough independent events/non-events | **Not satisfied** |

**Overall:** the original question is **not currently viable with existing evidence**. It is conditionally viable only if specific missing evidence becomes available: independent multi-year biology with non-bloom sampling, time-stamped/sub-weekly sampling, depth alignment, event/non-event definitions, sensor comparability and a pre-approved matched-FAR design. It is **not realistically testable within current constraints** because no such evidence is available and no new dataset search/contact is authorised.

More sensor observations would not fix the problem. The bottleneck is independent biological events, non-event exposure, temporal resolution and measurement alignment.

## 4. Prior art and originality

The field already contains:

- high-frequency cyanobacterial early warning/forecasting (Recknagel et al. 2025; Fournier et al. 2024; Beal et al. 2021/2022);
- EWS for algal blooms evaluated with true/false alarm rates (Buelo et al. 2022);
- empirical EWS assessments showing low reliability and low agreement (Gsell et al. 2016; O’Brien et al. 2023);
- multivariate resilience/relationship indicators with scenario-dependent performance (Weinans et al. 2021);
- Mendota-specific high-frequency phycocyanin research (Carpenter et al. 2020/2022);
- sensor-validation studies showing weak/variable RFU–biovolume relationships (Cotterill et al. 2019; Levi et al. 2025).

Matched false-alarm rate is a quality requirement, not a novelty. The exact combination of persistent relationship changes, independent event adjudication, interval-censored onset and matched FAR in cyanobacteria is **not confirmed as novel**; full-text comparator gaps remain. A new algorithm or a different lake would not by itself establish originality.

## 6. Final recommendation

**CONTINUE — NARROWED RESEARCH QUESTION.**

The narrowed question:

> **How comparable are high-frequency surface sensor proxies (chlorophyll/phycocyanin fluorescence, DO, temperature) with independent microscopy-derived cyanobacteria biovolume in Lake Mendota and Buffalo Pound Lake, and how do agreement and data quality vary by season, sensor era, and depth alignment?**

This is a descriptive measurement/comparability study, not an early-warning validation. It uses existing verified data, addresses a core blocker, and can produce a defensible positive/negative/limited result without labels, matched-FAR claims or new access.

## 7. Strongest counterargument

The narrowed study may be too incremental for a top competition and cannot fully isolate depth effects: Mendota has no depth profile and Buffalo Pound has only two seasons. If the team requires a genuinely original early-warning result, the current evidence cannot deliver it; **PAUSE** would be more honest than overclaiming. The narrowed study is scientifically defensible, but its originality is moderate/low.
