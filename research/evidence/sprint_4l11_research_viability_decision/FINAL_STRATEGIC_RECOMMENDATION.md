# Final Strategic Recommendation

**Gate 2:** PAUSED / NOT APPROVED — Option C (Exploratory Only)  
**Assessment date:** 2026-11-24

# CONTINUE — NARROWED RESEARCH QUESTION

The project should continue with a **narrowed measurement/comparability question**, not the original early-warning question. The original question is preserved as a long-term scientific aspiration but is **not currently testable**.

## 1. Why this is the strongest decision

- The original question fails on biological event/non-event validity, temporal resolution, measurement comparability, matched-FAR evaluation and statistical feasibility.
- Buffalo Pound has only two biological seasons (42 date-level observations), no non-bloom exposure and no documented researcher reply.
- Mendota is retained for limited exploratory use but rejected for early-warning validation: 14-day biology, no labels, 0.5 m vs 0–8 m depth mismatch, Turner/YSI discontinuity and incomplete QC.
- No other candidate is confirmatory-ready.
- Prior art already contains high-frequency cyanobacterial early warning/forecasting, EWS tests with true/false alarm rates, and multivariate indicator evaluations. Matched FAR is a quality requirement, not a novelty.
- A narrowed measurement question can be answered with existing verified data without new access, labels, models or contacts.
- It addresses the actual blocker — whether surface sensor proxies can represent independent cyanobacteria biovolume — and can produce a defensible positive/negative/limited result.

## 2. The narrowed question

> **How comparable are high-frequency surface sensor proxies (chlorophyll/phycocyanin fluorescence, DO, temperature) with independent microscopy-derived cyanobacteria biovolume in Lake Mendota and Buffalo Pound Lake, and how do agreement and data quality vary by season, sensor era, and depth alignment?**

**Scope:**
- descriptive/comparability study only;
- use existing verified Mendota and Buffalo Pound data;
- separate Turner and YSI eras; no cross-era concatenation;
- separate 0–8 m Mendota composites from 0.8 m Buffalo Pound samples;
- stratify by season and QC status;
- report uncertainty and negative/limited findings;
- no bloom labels, event intervals, matched-FAR claims or predictive models.

**Evidence base:**
- Mendota: 402 biology dates; 128 Turner chlorophyll and 139 Turner phycocyanin same-day unflagged dates; 21/22 YSI; 0.5 m sensors vs 0–8 m composite.
- Buffalo Pound: 42 microscopy dates (2018–2019) at 0.8 m; buoy sensors at 0.8 m; better depth alignment but only two seasons.
- This natural contrast can test whether depth alignment is associated with better sensor–biovolume agreement, with cross-lake confounding stated.

## 3. Evidence supporting the decision

- The original question is not testable with current evidence: 0 independently reconstructed bloom events, 0 defensible non-bloom intervals, no approved event definition, no matched-FAR denominator.
- Buffalo Pound remains pending; no reply or additional data are documented. Mendota was explicitly rejected for validation in Sprint 4L.10.
- Prior art shows high-frequency HAB early warning is already active (Recknagel 2025; Fournier 2024), EWS for algal blooms has already been evaluated with TPR/FPR (Buelo 2022), and empirical EWS often fail (Gsell 2016; O’Brien 2023).
- Sensor-validation literature shows RFU proxies are weak/variable and sensor-specific (Cotterill 2019; Levi 2025; USGS TM1-D10), making a measurement study scientifically meaningful.
- The project’s data audits are strong and reproducible; a narrowed study can use them without new acquisition.

## 4. Strongest counterargument

The narrowed question may be **too incremental** for a top competition. Sensor–biovolume comparability is already studied, and the project cannot fully isolate depth effects because Mendota has no depth profile and Buffalo Pound has only two seasons. A judge may ask why this is a new contribution rather than a local validation. If the team’s primary goal is a genuinely original early-warning result, neither the original nor the narrowed question delivers it with current evidence; in that case, **PAUSE** would be more honest than stretching the measurement study into an early-warning claim.

## 5. What would change the decision

Change toward **CONTINUE — ORIGINAL RESEARCH QUESTION** only if:

- independent biological data across multiple years, including non-bloom periods, become available;
- sampling is time-stamped and frequent enough to bound onset/termination below the claimed lead time;
- depth/spatial alignment and sensor comparability are documented;
- a biological-only event/non-event definition is approved;
- adequate events/non-events exist for matched-FAR evaluation;
- a leakage-resistant protocol is approved.

Change toward **PAUSE** if:

- no documented Buffalo Pound response or additional biological data arrive;
- the narrowed measurement question cannot be made sufficiently original/defensible;
- competition deadlines require a positive early-warning result that the evidence cannot support;
- the team cannot commit to a strict descriptive protocol.

A **PIVOT** is not recommended absent a specific, evidence-based opportunity and explicit approval; the current evidence does not identify a stronger alternative.

## 6. Next single project milestone

**Draft a one-page narrowed-question protocol and pre-analysis plan for human review.**

It should specify:

- the exact measurement/comparability question;
- Mendota and Buffalo Pound inclusion/exclusion rules;
- Turner vs YSI era separation;
- Mendota 0–8 m composite vs 0.5 m sensor and Buffalo Pound 0.8 m vs 0.8 m handling;
- seasonal and QC stratification;
- descriptive statistics/uncertainty;
- explicit non-early-warning framing;
- no labels, models, matched-FAR claims or protocol changes without approval.

**Do not begin analysis, modelling or competition narrative until that protocol is reviewed.** Gate 2 remains **PAUSED / NOT APPROVED — Option C**. This recommendation does not approve Gate 2, create labels, change the research question, acquire data or authorise external contact.
