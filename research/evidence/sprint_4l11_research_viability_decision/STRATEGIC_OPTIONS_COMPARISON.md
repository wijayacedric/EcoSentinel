# Strategic Options Comparison

**Gate 2:** PAUSED / NOT APPROVED — Option C (Exploratory Only)  
**Assessment date:** 2026-11-24

## 1. Decision criteria

Each option is assessed against:

- scientific validity and testability;
- evidence feasibility under current constraints;
- originality / differentiation;
- competition potential;
- risk of overclaiming or wasted effort;
- compatibility with Gate 2 and project governance.

## 2. Option A — Preserve the original research question

**Description:** Keep the question unchanged and seek the evidence needed to test it properly.

**What would be required:**
- independent multi-year biological event/non-event data;
- time-stamped, sub-weekly/event-triggered sampling;
- depth-resolved or explicitly matched biological samples;
- pre-approved biological event/non-event definition;
- adequate event count and non-event exposure;
- sensor cross-calibration, QC and maintenance history;
- leakage-resistant matched-FAR evaluation.

**Current feasibility:** **Low.** Buffalo Pound remains pending with no documented reply; public biology covers only 2018–2019 (42 dates) and no non-bloom season. Mendota is rejected for validation. No new dataset search/contact is authorised. Even additional Buffalo Pound years might not provide the temporal resolution needed for few-day lead times.

**Scientific validity if evidence existed:** High in principle; the question is meaningful and the evaluation design is appropriate. But it is not testable now.

**Risk:** Continuing to polish methods, synthetic tests or proxy analyses while the core evidence is absent; overclaiming; spending competition time on an untestable question.

**Verdict:** **Do not continue as the primary current project.** Preserve the question as a long-term aspiration; reconsider only if the specific evidence package becomes available and is approved.

## 3. Option B — Narrow the research question

**Proposed narrowed question:**

> How comparable are high-frequency surface sensor proxies (chlorophyll/phycocyanin fluorescence, DO, temperature) with independent microscopy-derived cyanobacteria biovolume in Lake Mendota and Buffalo Pound Lake, and how do agreement and data quality vary by season, sensor era, and depth alignment?

**Why this question:**
- It addresses the measurement-validity blocker directly.
- It uses existing, verified data: Mendota 402 biology dates, 128/139 same-day Turner proxies, 21/22 YSI; Buffalo Pound 42 dates at 0.8 m with 0.8 m sensors.
- It exploits a natural contrast: Buffalo Pound has approximately matched sensor–microscopy depth (0.8 m vs 0.8 m), while Mendota has a surface sensor (0.5 m) vs 0–8 m composite. This can test whether depth alignment matters, with caveats.
- It does not require bloom/non-bloom labels, matched FAR, or early-warning claims.
- It can produce a defensible positive/negative/limited measurement result.

**Scientific validity:** **Partially to fully feasible as a descriptive measurement study.** It must be framed as descriptive/comparability, not sensor validation for all conditions. It needs pre-specified inclusion rules, era separation, seasonal stratification, uncertainty, and depth caveats.

**Originality:** **Moderate/low.** Sensor validation literature exists; the two-lake depth/era contrast is a modest local contribution. It is not an algorithmic or early-warning novelty. It can be scientifically useful, but not a flagship “new early-warning system.”

**Competition potential:** **Moderate.** A rigorous measurement/negative study can be competitive at SciFest/GENIUS if well presented; less aligned with Earth Prize’s solution/innovation emphasis; ISEF/EUCYS eligibility unknown. It is scientifically honest but may be less exciting than a validated early-warning system.

**Risk:** Incremental relative to Cotterill/Levi/USGS; cross-lake differences (sensor types, taxonomy, protocols) confound the depth contrast; Mendota 0.5 m vs 0–8 m cannot isolate depth without profiles; Buffalo Pound 42 dates limits precision.

**Verdict:** **Strongest realistic route.** It is the only option that is scientifically defensible, feasible with existing data, and honest about not validating early warning.

## 4. Option C — Change the research direction (pivot)

**Description:** Adopt a different environmental research question with a stronger combination of originality, validity, feasibility and competition potential.

**Assessment:** No specific pivot is justified by the current evidence. A new direction would require:
- a new research question and protocol;
- new data or a new access route;
- new literature positioning;
- likely a broad dataset search or external contact, both currently prohibited.

Possible adjacent topics (sensor QC/artifact detection; seasonal metabolism; depth-integrated monitoring) are either already covered by prior work or would be a new project without a demonstrated gap. A pivot to avoid the difficulty of the original question would violate the project’s evidence-first standard.

**Verdict:** **Not recommended.** Do not pivot without a specific, evidence-based opportunity and explicit approval.

## 5. Option D — Pause the project

**Description:** Stop active work until adequate evidence becomes available.

**Strengths:** Avoids overclaiming, wasted competition effort and repeated re-analysis of inadequate data. Honest if no defensible route exists.

**Weaknesses:** A feasible narrowed measurement study exists; pausing would discard a legitimate use of the existing evidence. It also provides no competition output.

**When pause would be preferable:** if the team cannot commit to a strict descriptive protocol, if competition deadlines require a positive early-warning result, or if no narrow question can be made sufficiently original/defensible.

**Verdict:** **Second-best fallback**, not the primary recommendation.

## 7. Decision

**CONTINUE — NARROWED RESEARCH QUESTION.**

The original question is preserved but not pursued as a current confirmatory project. The narrowed measurement/comparability question is the strongest realistic route because it is scientifically meaningful, uses existing verified evidence, addresses a core blocker, and does not pretend to validate early warning.
