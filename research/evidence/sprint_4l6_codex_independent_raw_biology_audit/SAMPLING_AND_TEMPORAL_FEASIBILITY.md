# Sampling and temporal feasibility

## Verified sampling cadence

The raw export has 402 Mendota dates from Jan 1995 through Dec 2020. Median date gap is 14 days, mean 23.57, max 120; 72 gaps at least 30 days, 35 at least 60, six at least 90. There are 13–19 dates per year, but months vary and samples continue into winter depending on ice conditions. EML's nominal protocol says biweekly during ice-free late March/early April to early September, every four weeks to late November, and usually one winter sample. Raw coverage broadly reflects this seasonal design but includes long gaps and anomalous dates; 14-day median is not regular sampling.

## Literature context

Hansen & Carey (PLOS ONE 2015, DOI `10.1371/journal.pone.0115414`) state samples from the deep hole were biweekly to monthly, integrated 0–8m, during open water; >400 natural units per sample were identified. They explicitly note phytoplankton turnover on weekly or shorter timescales and sampling error from patchiness/small water volumes. This is a strong warning against treating the 26-year span as dense temporal exposure.

## What timing can support

A sample date is a point measurement of a composite, not a continuously observed state. If a future, justified biological event definition is applied, first observed event-positive date and prior observed non-positive date would only bracket onset. The bracket width is the intersample gap and may be 14 days median but reaches 120 days; with no time-of-day data, sample and buoy alignment also cannot be exact. There are no event intervals in this audit.

- **Hours-to-days warning:** unsupported by this biological cadence; events may start and end between samples.
- **Multi-week warning:** potentially exploratory only where actual sampling exposure allows, with event onset interval censoring and no claim of precise lead time.
- **Seasonal/annual change:** descriptively feasible, but seasonal succession and variable month coverage confound raw date-to-date comparisons. Year-level aggregation has precedent in community studies but would not answer short-term early warning.

A long calendar span increases observed years, not number of independently adjudicated bloom onsets or valid negative exposure. No event counts were invented or calculated.
