# Mendota High-Frequency Sensor Monitoring Review

## Source base and coverage claims

NTL-LTER's [High Frequency Data](https://lter.limnology.wisc.edu/high-frequency-data/) and [Core Datasets](https://lter.limnology.wisc.edu/core-datasets/) pages describe the buoy and list Lake Mendota's high-frequency records. The core catalog lists Mendota multiparameter sonde profiles from 2017–current and the Madison phytoplankton record from 1995–current. The Mendota buoy page hosted by UW-Madison SSEC says the buoy is about 1.5 km northeast of Picnic Point and measures wind, air temperature, dew point/relative humidity, a vertical water-temperature profile, dissolved oxygen, chlorophyll and phycocyanin ([buoy overview](https://metobs.ssec.wisc.edu/mendota/buoy/)). These are catalog/program descriptions, not a guarantee that every variable is valid or continuously present through every stated year.

A primary sensor study (Carpenter et al. 2020, [DOI 10.1002/lol2.10152](https://doi.org/10.1002/lol2.10152)) directly reports that chlorophyll-a and phycocyanin were monitored each minute during ice-free seasons 2008–2018 at a central station, using Turner Designs Cyclops 7 fluorometers suspended 1.0 m below the buoy. Each ice-free season contributed roughly 175 days, about 250,000 minute observations. The paper states fluorescence was recorded in relative fluorescence units (RFU), directly related to pigment concentrations. This confirms a substantial high-frequency legacy time series, but not uninterrupted measurements at every minute: the paper's approximate counts are described as available time series, and QC/missingness must be evaluated from data and recordkeeping.

A later paper extends high-frequency phycocyanin analysis through 2021 using YSI EXO2 sensors at 1.0 m below the buoy and states that sensors were cleaned/calibrated every 1–2 weeks ([DOI 10.1002/ecs2.4903](https://doi.org/10.1002/ecs2.4903)). This adds method-specific evidence for the newer era, but does not establish homogeneity between Turner and YSI outputs. The NTL-LTER core program plan and sensor-list evidence document several transitions. The draft plan says the buoy's DO sensor experienced significant algal/zebra-mussel biofouling in 2017; a PME miniDOT replaced the D-Opto in 2018 for one year; a YSI EXO2 sonde was added in 2019 with DO, chlorophyll, phycocyanin, specific conductance, pH, fDOM and turbidity; the pigment channels replaced Turner Cyclops 7 fluorometers and have significant magnitude differences although both output RFU. The plan reports YSI pH/DO/conductivity cleaned and calibrated every two weeks in 2019 and nearly weekly in 2020. It also reports a 2017 boating mishap caused loss of meteorological sensors from May 28 to July 11, and CO2 non-operation July 2–September 17, 2020. See the [NTL Core Program Plan PDF](https://lter.limnology.wisc.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/2029/2023/05/LTER-Core-Program-Project-Plan-v1-DRAFT.pdf).

The inventory independently audited for Sprint 4L.2 (20 rows in the 2006-to-present sensor listing) identifies a 2007 replacement of the water-temperature instrument, wind replacement in 2013, DO generations across 2016–2019, a 2017 meteorological replacement, and 2019 addition/replacement of multiple sonde variables. It gives only year boundaries and “present,” not deployment dates, operating status, calibration, QC, serial numbers or variable-specific uptime. These previously verified inventory facts are used as project evidence; no raw sensor values were examined in this review.

## Predictor suitability and independence

### Potential environmental predictors

- **Water temperature:** long-running profile and a core environmental driver; likely strongest overlap with biology. Depth-specific time series and instrument-era comparability must be determined. A surface/epilimnetic signal does not automatically represent 0–8 m integrated biological conditions.
- **Dissolved oxygen:** ecologically informative but begins later in the inventory and has DO instrument changes plus documented biofouling. DO can also reflect photosynthesis/respiration and thus be a close biological response, not an exogenous driver. Its use may still be valid if treated as monitored state, but its lead signal should not be presented as causal forcing.
- **Chlorophyll-a and phycocyanin fluorescence:** useful operational monitoring proxies and plausible conventional comparators. Chlorophyll is broad algal pigment, not cyanobacteria-specific. Phycocyanin is diagnostically associated with cyanobacteria but RFU depends on pigment concentration and optical/instrument conditions and is not a species count or toxin measurement. These sensors are not suitable as the independent reference endpoint if the target event is defined from them.
- **pH, conductivity, turbidity, fDOM:** potentially useful environmental state variables, but buoy listing indicates only 2019 onward for several channels; if the biological package ends in 2020, nominal overlap is very short and may provide few independent seasons. They are outputs of combined biogeochemical and optical processes and are not necessarily independent drivers.
- **CO2 and PAR:** plausible process indicators, but coverage starts later and specific measurement meaning/location/height/depth and valid operating period require the relevant metadata. PAR above/below could support light attenuation only with simultaneous, geometry-matched, calibrated channels.
- **Air temperature, humidity, wind and radiation:** independent of the biological endpoint as atmospheric forcing, but meteorology may not be available at a lake-representative scale and wind instrument eras changed.

The biological microscopy endpoint and sensor channels are distinct sources, but predictors may be mechanistically downstream of or coupled to algal conditions. “Independent endpoint” should mean the event is adjudicated from microscopy without using any predictor channel. It does not mean predictor signals are statistically independent of biology.

## Point sensors, depth and spatial representativeness

The biology described in primary studies is a 0–8 m composite from the deep hole. The 2020 pigment study describes fluorometers 1.0 m below the buoy. The EML metadata inspected during Sprint 4L.2 lists pigment depth as 0.5 m. These source descriptions conflict, possibly because of deployment era, reference datum, or inventory differences; this review cannot resolve the discrepancy. Until period-specific deployment metadata clarify depth, do not state a single sensor depth as universal.

The buoy is central/deep-water, and the LTER sample site is the deep hole. Their nominal spatial proximity supports a plausible paired-site analysis, but co-location should not be assumed at exact coordinates, and one central station cannot represent shoreline accumulation, beaches, or lake-wide scums. A vertical temperature array has a depth profile; other channels are point measurements. Sensor depth may be fixed, adjusted with water level, or changed with deployment; retrieve time-specific placement.

## Instrument eras and discontinuity risk

The largest documented multi-variable transition is 2019. Chlorophyll/phycocyanin moved from Turner Cyclops to YSI EXO2, and the plan explicitly says output magnitudes differ significantly despite both being RFU. Joining these as a continuous homogeneous predictor without cross-calibration or segmentation risks a level shift or altered variance that could itself look like relationship change. DO also spans D-Opto, miniDOT and EXO2; wind, air temperature/humidity, and water-temperature sensors changed. The 2017 buoy loss and documented DO biofouling show that availability and quality vary within an instrument era.

Methodological implications are serious for a relationship-change detector: a shared sensor transition can perturb multiple predictors at once and create artificial covariance/decoupling. A detector using rolling correlations or another network statistic may react strongly to common maintenance, standardized scale differences, calibration drift, seasonal deployment starts/ends, missing-value patterns, or changed sampling frequency. The issue is not resolved by high nominal temporal resolution. Cross-era claims need versioned calibrations, QA/QC flags, overlap/intercomparison results and analysis support restricted to well-documented homogeneous periods.

## Quality control and missingness

Public program documents reveal some calibration/cleaning cadence and specific incidents, but this is not a full QA/QC specification. This review did not read raw values or construct missingness statistics. Needed: timestamp conventions/time zone; variable names/units and conversions; native and archived sampling intervals; pre/post filtering; manual flags; fouling/cleaning logs; calibration solutions and coefficients; spikes, drift, sensor swaps; data gaps; physical plausibility filters; and quality-control edits. Do not infer availability or valid negative exposure from deployment-years or “present.”

## Relevant predictor families for 88.31

Potential pairing classes are temperature, DO, chlorophyll/phycocyanin, meteorology, and (for later overlap) pH/conductivity/turbidity/fDOM/CO2/PAR. Only temperature and meteorology have the plausible long temporal coverage; actual overlap of values with 88.31 is not established here. The user's package version must be checked for actual end date. If it is version 31 with a cutoff near 2020, variables first listed from 2019 contribute only nominal 2019–2020 overlap. This is not a verified analysis interval.

## Suitability conclusion

High-frequency sensors are scientifically useful as predictors and comparators, with the strongest potential for physical, meteorological and pigment monitoring. They cannot independently ground-truth a bloom event. There is strong prior evidence of useful high-frequency signal, but the instrument history, sensor depth conflict, explicit fouling/coverage problems, 2019 scale discontinuity, and point-versus-integrated biology mismatch preclude a simple homogeneous long-term multivariate record. Sensor suitability is **conditionally supported for exploratory and era-aware work** and **unresolved for confirmatory multi-era evaluation** until data/QC/metadata are audited.
