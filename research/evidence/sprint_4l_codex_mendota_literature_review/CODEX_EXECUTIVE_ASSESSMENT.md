## Executive verdict

**Verdict: Retain Lake Mendota for exploratory analysis only; it is a scientifically credible candidate for further verification, but the evidence reviewed here does not support confirmatory Gate 2 readiness.** This is a positive assessment of scientific relevance, not authorization to proceed.

Mendota has unusually valuable paired evidence in principle: long-term microscopy-based phytoplankton observations, including cyanobacterial taxa, alongside high-frequency buoy measurements. The NTL-LTER program documents phytoplankton biovolume monitoring and regular summer sampling; published work reports species-level microscopy, cell densities, and calculated biovolumes from an integrated 0–8 m sample. High-frequency buoy studies document minute-resolution chlorophyll-a and phycocyanin fluorescence for ice-free seasons, and a much longer water-temperature record. These are independent measurement modalities in the relevant sense: microscopy is not computed from buoy fluorescence. They can support an independently measured biological abundance endpoint after the exact EDI package version, variables, taxonomic crosswalk, and protocol history are verified.

The principal scientific limitation is temporal: biological samples are snapshots, typically biweekly in spring/summer and monthly outside summer, while sensor records are minute-scale, seasonal, and contain gaps. Brief events can start and resolve between biological visits. Onset will generally be interval-censored; an apparently quiet sampled date cannot prove the entire intervening interval was bloom-free. That cadence is poorly matched to claims of warning by hours or a few days. It may be adequate to ask a coarser, prespecified multi-week question, subject to event dates and exposures actually recoverable from the authorized biological audit. The endpoint must be defined from independent microscopy abundance/biovolume and an independently justified criterion. Taxon presence alone is not a bloom, microscopy abundance is not toxicity, and phycocyanin is a pigment proxy rather than taxonomic ground truth.

There is substantial prior art. Mendota has published seasonal and subseasonal cyanobacteria forecasting, hour-to-month algal forecasting, daily/weekly lagged analyses of phycocyanin against environmental forcings, and high-frequency stochastic dynamics studies. Independent empirical-lake literature already compares univariate and multivariate early-warning methods and warns that performance can be processing-dependent and weak. The novelty cannot be “using high-frequency data,” “using multivariate indicators,” or “matching false-alarm rates.” A possible contribution is narrower: a prespecified, fair head-to-head evaluation of a particular environmental relationship-change signal against clearly defined conventional thresholds and persistence baselines, using the same independently measured microscopy endpoint, valid prediction origins, horizon, exposure, and false-alarm accounting. A literature review cannot establish that contribution without the exact proposed indicator and a complete, method-specific bibliography of directly comparable studies. The exact EcoSentinel detector specification was not available in this assignment, so this part remains incomplete.

Several comparability and validation blockers remain. The biology is a 0–8 m integrated sample at the deep-hole station; pigment sensors are a point measurement suspended near the surface, and published descriptions give differing sensor depths (1.0 m in the 2020 paper versus 0.5 m in metadata accessed for the sensor inventory). Buoy records cross instrument transitions, including a major 2019 replacement of pigment and several sonde channels. A sensor inventory lists years, not uninterrupted valid observations, calibration equivalence, or QC. Ecological processes also shift: published Mendota analyses document changes in phytoplankton phenology and invasion-associated changes in Cyanobacteria timing. These all create plausible non-biological or nonstationary shifts in cross-variable relationships.

No arbitrary event-count cutoff is proposed. The capacity for a meaningful evaluation depends on a blinded, pre-analysis census of independently adjudicable events, non-event time, missingness, onset interval widths, and independent years, followed by precision/sensitivity calculations that preserve the proposed estimand. That census is not performed here. If only a small number of usable events or too little non-bloom exposure remain after eligibility rules, a single-lake confirmatory performance claim will not be supported; additional lakes or an exploratory claim would be required through an authorized protocol decision.

## Key conclusions

1. **Scientific credibility:** yes, as an exploratory candidate and potentially as a coarse-horizon validation site, pending package-level biological audit and event definition.
2. **Strongest contribution:** independent microscopy endpoint paired with much denser environmental histories to test whether a specified relationship-change alert adds warning lead time over conventional monitoring at a common operating point.
3. **Most serious limitations:** sparse/seasonally uneven biology, interval-censored or missed onsets, lack of an existing independent bloom-event adjudication, point-versus-integrated depth mismatch, major sensor-era changes, spatial representativeness, and likely limited independent event count.
4. **Prior-art overlap:** material. Mendota forecasting and sensor-dynamics work is extensive; broad EWS literature already compares multivariate with univariate approaches. A defensible gap remains possible only after a precise novelty comparison.
5. **Gate 2 evidence needed:** verified exact package contents/version and methods history; biological endpoint and observation-effort rules; matched time/depth/location QC records; sensor maintenance/calibration/transition record; event-neutral exposure; viable held-out-year design; event-count and uncertainty feasibility; exact literature novelty review.
6. **Resolvable vs fundamental:** metadata, calibration and overlap uncertainties may be resolvable. Sparse biology, missed short events, point-to-composite mismatch, and only one lake are structural limitations that cannot be repaired by statistical processing. Their practical impact depends on the forecast horizon and claim.

## Evidence categories and confidence

- **Verified primary/institutional facts:** NTL-LTER's current public overview describes summer biweekly and non-summer monthly sampling and phytoplankton biovolume availability for Mendota/Monona; current core-dataset listing identifies the Madison phytoplankton and Mendota sonde records; NTL-LTER explains EDI package access now requires sign-in. Peer-reviewed studies directly describe legacy Mendota methods and buoy sensor periods.
- **Interpretations:** those sources establish strong potential biological independence, but not that every record in version 88.31 is comparable or that a bloom endpoint is already validated. Sampling frequency likely cannot resolve onset on a daily scale.
- **Uncertainties:** package-version-specific taxa, exact dates/fields/units, method changes in the full record, detection/quantification limits, sample-specific taxonomist and count uncertainty, years and seasons with usable overlap, and current sensor QC remain unverified in this review.

## Evidence limitations

The public EDI landing page for `knb-lter-ntl.88.31` returned HTTP 403 in the research browser. NTL-LTER's own Core Datasets page says sign-in is required to access package metadata and data. In keeping with the instruction not to duplicate OpenCode's acquisition work or download additional datasets, this review did not attempt another acquisition. We therefore do not assert file-level facts for version 88.31 from the package itself. We use NTL-LTER's public documentation and peer-reviewed descriptions of related record intervals, and flag version-specific claims for verification. Sensor instrument-era statements are supported by the project plan and the independent Sprint 4L.2 Codex review of the sensor inventory, not by raw sensor-value analysis.
